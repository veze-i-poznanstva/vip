# Degrowth License - Preamble

## Purpose and Philosophy

This license exists because the dominant economic system pursues endless growth on a finite planet, driving us past critical planetary boundaries through extractivism, artificial scarcity, and the concentration of wealth and power. Technology and creative production have become infrastructure for this system—optimizing consumption, enabling surveillance capitalism, and locking knowledge behind proprietary walls that prevent repair, adaptation, and genuine innovation.

Traditional open source licensing has enabled extraordinary knowledge sharing and collaborative development. Yet its unconditional openness has enabled uses that drive ecological destruction: permissive licenses became infrastructure for surveillance capitalism, platform monopolies, and growth-optimizing systems. We build on open source's collaborative ethos while adding ecological boundaries that earlier licenses did not contemplate.

Freedom means more than the liberty to buy and sell. True freedom includes the freedom to understand and modify the technologies we depend on, the freedom to participate democratically in decisions affecting our lives and workplaces, the freedom to live within ecological limits, and the freedom of future generations to inherit a habitable planet. Freedom requires material security, time for meaningful activity beyond wage labor, and autonomy from coercive systems that force participation in ecological destruction.

All knowledge builds on collective inheritance. Every line of code, every design, every creative work rests on shared languages, protocols, and insights contributed by millions. Proprietary enclosure obscures this reality, falsely attributing innovation to individual genius while extracting rent from collective knowledge.

## What This License Enables

This license protects creative and technical works as commons while directing their use toward ecological regeneration and social equity. It enables and encourages:

- **Convivial technology**: Software, designs, and tools that increase human autonomy, support mutual aid, and can be understood, modified, and repaired by their users
- **Open knowledge and creativity**: Designs, documentation, creative works, hardware specifications, and datasets that can be freely studied, adapted, and shared
- **Degrowth economics**: Applications supporting human needs while respecting planetary boundaries through extended lifespans, sharing, repair, and reduced material throughput
- **Ecological restoration**: Tools for ecosystem regeneration, appropriate-scale energy systems, agroecology, production for sufficiency rather than growth, and community resilience
- **Democratic governance**: Works that strengthen cooperative ownership, participatory decision-making, and challenge concentrated power
- **Knowledge commons**: Creative and technical works that treat information as shared wealth rather than extractable resource
- **Community and conviviality**: Tools supporting local cooperation, time banking, skill sharing, community care, and relationships rooted in sufficiency rather than endless consumption
- **Education and learning**: Licensed works are **always freely available for educational purposes, research, personal learning, and skill development** without restriction

## Prohibited Uses

This license specifically prohibits use of licensed works in the following categories:

- **Fossil supply expansion**: Exploration, new extraction infrastructure, or capacity increase in fossil fuel operations (identifiable through NAICS/SIC codes, project documentation, or operational purpose)
- **Luxury consumption**: Goods or services designed for elite consumption, including private aviation, super-yachts, and similar luxury markets that serve status signaling rather than genuine needs
- **Planned obsolescence and anti-repair**: Digital rights management preventing repair, parts pairing that restricts independent service, warranty voiding for user maintenance, or any design deliberately shortening product lifespan
- **Surveillance, advertising technology, and behavioral manipulation**: User profiling for targeted advertising, emotion inference systems, workplace micro-monitoring, or technologies designed to manipulate behavior for commercial gain
- **Throughput-dependent business models**: Operations whose viability explicitly requires continuous demand growth, including growth-pegged financing covenants or performance metrics requiring perpetual volume expansion
- **Extractive finance**: High-frequency trading, predatory lending, payday lending, or exploitative buy-now-pay-later schemes

These prohibitions apply regardless of ownership structure. A worker-owned cooperative engaged in fossil fuel extraction cannot use works under this license.

## Excluded Practices: False Solutions

This license excludes practices that have demonstrated systemic rebound effects or prevent genuine transformation:

- **Green growth**: The claim of endless GDP expansion while decoupling from ecological destruction. Absolute decoupling at required scale has never been demonstrated and is inconsistent with biophysical evidence at required scales.
- **Techno-salvation**: Reliance on innovation alone without fundamental changes to production, consumption, and power. No amount of technological sophistication can substitute for reduced throughput and democratic transformation.
- **Escapism**: Mars colonization or geoengineering proposals that externalize responsibility rather than confronting planetary limits.
- **Efficiency traps**: Where efficiency gains drive increased consumption (Jevons paradox) rather than reduced impact, when embedded in growth-oriented systems.

Works supporting these approaches cannot use this license. This license supports technology and creativity serving degrowth transformation, not perpetuation of growth ideology with green aesthetics.

We acknowledge the tension in creating boundaries around commons. Successful commons, as Elinor Ostrom demonstrated, require governance structures and use rules decided by commoners themselves. These boundaries aim to protect commons from enclosure by capital, not to restrict commoners from meeting genuine needs.

These restrictions represent a **net de-enclosure** compared to proprietary regimes that create artificial scarcity of knowledge. We are not claiming moral purity, but making a collective choice about which uses of shared knowledge we will support.

## On North-South Equity

We recognize that ecological overshoot is primarily driven by high-income countries that have built wealth through extraction and emissions. This license should not become a tool of eco-colonialism restricting the Global South's access to technology for meeting genuine human needs.

We use "Global North" and "Global South" as imperfect shorthand. The real distinctions are not geographic but about historical extraction, cumulative emissions, and current consumption levels. A poor Eastern European country that wasn't a colonial power should not face the same restrictions as wealthy post-colonial states. Conversely, wealthy elites anywhere—including in the Global South—engaging in luxury consumption or extractivism are not exempted by geography.

The key criteria are: Does this use meet genuine human needs? Does it respect planetary boundaries? Does it address historical deprivation or perpetuate extraction? These questions must be answered contextually, not by simplistic geographic categories.

Uses addressing poverty, healthcare, education, food security, clean water, and renewable infrastructure are encouraged regardless of temporary increases in material throughput, especially in contexts of historical deprivation. We firmly oppose luxury consumption and extractivism anywhere, while supporting development that enhances capabilities within ecological limits.

The definition of "degrowth-compatible use" must be determined through global dialogue that centers voices from communities facing both ecological crisis and economic marginalization.

## Enforcement: Legal and Cultural

This license relies on multiple enforcement mechanisms:

- **Automated compliance tools** that flag unapproved licenses in supply chains, creating organizational friction
- **Whistleblowers and community monitoring** documenting violations  
- **Reputational consequences** within technical and degrowth communities
- **Traditional copyright enforcement** where feasible, though we acknowledge the practical and financial barriers to litigation. Disputes are subject to the jurisdiction of Ecuador's courts, which uniquely recognize Rights of Nature (Pachamama) in their constitution. This forum selection aligns with the license's ecological values and provides a legal framework that can interpret ambiguous terms through the lens of planetary boundaries rather than solely through commercial or corporate interests.

We are honest that vague ecological criteria create enforcement challenges. This is acceptable because **the primary function of this license is cultural and political**: It sparks dialogue about technology's role in ecological crisis, builds community among those committed to planetary boundaries, and creates legal friction for extractive uses even when perfect enforcement is impossible.

## Production Ethics and Systemic Transformation
This license focuses on restricting uses of commons rather than regulating production conditions. This is a pragmatic limitation: supply chain verification creates enforcement barriers that would exclude small and marginalized creators, contradicting our accessibility and equity commitments.

However, we acknowledge degrowth requires examining total provisioning systems - not only what we make, but how we make it. Contributors are encouraged to consider: energy sources, labor conditions, material extraction, and ecological impacts of their creative practices. Community governance may develop approaches to production ethics in future versions.

True transformation requires changing both what technology does (use restrictions) and how it's created (production conditions). This license addresses the former; movements for labor justice, renewable energy transitions, and supply chain accountability address the latter. Both are necessary; neither is sufficient alone.

## The Horizon We Move Toward

We envision a world where technology serves human flourishing within ecological limits rather than endless accumulation. Where our creative and technical works enable cooperation rather than extraction, transparency rather than control, sufficiency rather than perpetual growth. Where knowledge is treated as commons—accessible to all who would use it to meet genuine needs, heal ecosystems, and build democratic alternatives.

We draw inspiration from frameworks like Kate Raworth's Doughnut Economics, which envisions a safe and just space between social foundations and ecological ceilings. While the Doughnut framework is deliberately growth-agnostic, this license takes a clear stance: in high-income countries that have vastly exceeded planetary boundaries, continued growth in material and energy throughput is incompatible with ecological stability. We support meeting human needs and expanding capabilities, but reject the ideology that this requires endless GDP expansion.

We envision communities oriented around care, craft, conviviality, and meaningful work rather than consumption and status competition. Lives rich in relationships, skills, creativity, and connection to place and nature—not in accumulation of commodities designed to break. Time for rest, learning, political participation, and the cultivation of human capacities beyond what markets value.

This is not austerity or deprivation but abundance of what truly matters: autonomy, community, meaningful work, ecological health, and time for being human rather than being productive. The research is clear that beyond basic material security, wellbeing comes from strong relationships, purposeful activity, connection to nature, and democratic participation—none of which require perpetual growth.

This license is one small tool in that larger transformation. Its legal function matters less than its role in building movements, sparking conversations, and demonstrating that another relationship between technology, commons, and ecology is possible.

This license expires January 1, 2050. The exact date is less important than the scale: we have approximately one generation to transform how we meet human needs, or climate feedback loops render our choices irrelevant. Every project under this license carries that countdown. We cannot impose 2025 frameworks on 2050 conditions. Degrowth is a transition strategy toward sufficiency within limits, not a permanent restriction regime. If transformation succeeds, these restrictions become less necessary. If it fails, they become meaningless. Either way, future communities—not us—will respond to their realities. We act decisively within our window.


## Openness & Compatibility

**Status**: This is not "open source" as defined by the Open Source Initiative, nor "free software" under the Free Software Foundation's four freedoms, nor "open hardware" as defined by various hardware certification bodies. It is a **source-available, purpose-restricted commons license** aligned with degrowth principles.

We prioritize ecological boundaries and democratic values over definitional purity. The restrictions we impose violate OSI criteria specifically because we believe some uses (fossil fuel extraction, planned obsolescence, extractive surveillance) should not have unrestricted access to commons, even if that means we cannot use the "open source" or "free cultural works" labels.

**Share-Alike Requirement**: Any work that incorporates, modifies, or builds upon material licensed under this license must itself be licensed under the Degrowth License or a compatible license with equivalent restrictions. This copyleft provision prevents proprietary enclosure of the commons and ensures that improvements remain accessible under the same ecological boundaries. You cannot take from this commons and contribute to extraction—if you use degrowth-licensed works, you commit your derivative works to the same principles.

**Compatibility**: Works under this license cannot be combined with GPL, Apache, MIT, Creative Commons, or most other permissive licenses due to our additional restrictions. This will limit adoption in projects with complex dependency chains. We accept this limitation as necessary to maintain ecological boundaries.

This license may be combined with other ethical licenses to address additional concerns, provided such restrictions do not contradict degrowth principles.

**Why we accept this trade-off**: We believe protecting commons from destructive uses serves degrowth values more than maximizing adoption through permissive licensing.

## 4. EQUITY OVERRIDE: CONTRACTION & CONVERGENCE

We recognize ecological overshoot as driven by historically wealthy regions and current elites who have exceeded planetary boundaries through extraction, not by peoples meeting basic needs or recovering from colonial suppression.

**Framework:** Those with greatest ecological footprint and historical responsibility must **contract** material and energy use fastest. Those below social foundations may temporarily **expand** provisioning of genuine needs, with support. All must **converge** within planetary boundaries. This is not "green growth"—we reject the fantasy of infinite GDP expansion with decoupling. This is sufficiency: meeting needs within limits.

### Who This Applies To

**Notwithstanding Section 3**, uses are permitted by:

- **Peoples and communities with low ecological footprint** - where per capita material consumption, energy use, and emissions remain well within planetary fair share

- **Contexts of historical colonial extraction** - regions that were colonized, subjected to resource extraction, or whose development was suppressed by imperial powers, now building autonomous capacity

- **Indigenous peoples** exercising self-determination, traditional rights, and sovereignty over their territories

- **Worker cooperatives, solidarity economy networks, and mutual aid organizations** primarily serving marginalized communities and operating within or transitioning toward ecological limits

- **Projects addressing basic capabilities** - health, clean water, sanitation, nutrition, housing, education, energy access, mobility for essential needs, democratic participation, care systems

### Assessment Criteria: Capabilities, Not GDP

We explicitly reject GDP/GNI-based classifications (World Bank income categories, etc.) as growth-fundamentalist metrics. Instead, assess based on:

**1. Historical Responsibility**  
Has this region/community/group contributed disproportionately to ecological overshoot and extraction? Or suffered from others' extraction and been denied development autonomy?

**2. Current Ecological Footprint**  
Does this use keep people within fair share of planetary boundaries? Or does it perpetuate overconsumption? Look at per capita material throughput, energy use, emissions—not monetary income.

**3. Colonial & Post-Colonial Context**  
Is this occurring in context of ongoing decolonization, recovering from imperial extraction, exercising newly-won or long-denied sovereignty, or resisting ongoing extractive pressures?

**4. Genuine Needs vs. Manufactured Wants**  
Does this address basic human capabilities (Nussbaum/Sen framework: health, nutrition, shelter, education, political voice, affiliation, play, control over environment)? Or manufacture artificial scarcity and luxury markets?

**5. Trajectory & Transition**  
Is this building capacity for ecological transition toward sufficiency? Or replicating high-throughput extractive patterns? Is there a credible pathway to converge within limits?

**6. Provisioning Efficiency**  
How many genuine needs are met per unit of energy/material throughput? Degrowth measures success here, not total output.

### Where Such Use Must:

- **Address genuine subsistence needs or redress historical deprivation** - closing capability gaps, not mimicking Northern overconsumption

- **Build local capacity for ecological transition** - renewable energy, agroecology, repair networks, knowledge commons, democratic infrastructure

- **NOT primarily serve export to high-consumption regions** - meeting local/regional needs, not feeding Global North consumption

- **NOT replicate extractive patterns** - even at local scale, luxury consumption by elites anywhere remains prohibited under Section 3(d)

- **Operate within or credibly transition toward planetary boundaries** - temporary expansion of material throughput to meet basic needs is acceptable; permanent high-throughput replication of Northern models is not

### Interpretation: Power, Ecology, History Over Borders

**Geography alone doesn't determine eligibility.** 

- A billionaire's luxury compound in Kenya: **Prohibited** (high footprint, luxury consumption, elite)
- A solar cooperative in Rwanda meeting energy needs: **Permitted** (low footprint, genuine needs, post-colonial capacity building)
- Indigenous territory in Canada: **Permitted** (low footprint despite location in "wealthy" country, ongoing colonialism, self-determination)
- Wealthy suburban development in any country: **Prohibited** (high footprint, manufactured wants, perpetuates overshoot)

**Context matters more than nominal country classification.** Ask:
- What is the **per capita ecological footprint** of those benefiting?
- What is their **historical relationship to colonial extraction**?
- Are we **expanding capabilities within limits** or replicating consumption patterns that created crisis?
- Is this **contraction** (reducing excess), **convergence** (expanding toward sufficiency), or **overshoot** (exceeding boundaries)?

### No "Green Growth" Loophole

This override does NOT permit:
- Luxury consumption anywhere ("we're a developing country so our billionaires can have private jets")
- Planned obsolescence anywhere ("we need GDP growth first, then we'll care about repair")
- Surveillance capitalism anywhere ("we need data extractivism to develop")
- Fossil fuel expansion anywhere ("we need cheap energy for development")

**All Section 3 Prohibited Uses remain prohibited everywhere.** This override permits temporary increases in **essential provisioning throughput** while building **ecological transition capacity**. It does not permit replicating the extractive, high-throughput, luxury-consumption models that caused overshoot.

### Solidarity & Support (Principle, Not Yet Mechanism)

Those with high historical responsibility and current capacity should actively support low-footprint essential-needs projects through: technology transfer, hosting/infrastructure support, knowledge sharing, financial solidarity, skills exchange, or other forms of mutual aid.

DACL v0.1 establishes this as **ethical expectation** for high-capacity users. Future versions may develop mechanisms (certification, community review, solidarity obligations). For now: if you benefit from high-throughput infrastructure built on historical extraction, consider what you owe to those building alternatives.

## 6. COMPATIBILITY & LICENSE RELATIONSHIPS

### DACL is Incompatible With:

- **Permissive licenses** (MIT, Apache, BSD): They allow any use including Prohibited Uses
- **GPL family** (GPLv2, GPLv3, AGPL): They require freedom for any purpose including Prohibited Uses  
- **Creative Commons** (CC-BY, CC-BY-SA): No use restrictions
- **Proprietary licenses**: Obviously

Why? These licenses grant permissions we explicitly prohibit. You cannot simultaneously permit fossil fuel use (GPL) and prohibit it (DACL). Combining them is logical impossibility.

**Interoperability without mixing:** While you cannot combine DACL code with incompatible licenses in a single work, different licenses can interoperate through APIs, network protocols, and defined interfaces. A DACL service can communicate with GPL software through APIs without license conflict, as long as codebases remain separate.

### DACL-Compatible Licenses:

Currently: Other versions of DACL, licenses explicitly approved by DACL Steward governance.

Potentially: Future ecological licenses with equivalent or stronger restrictions aligned with degrowth principles.

We expect the compatible license list to grow through community governance.

### One-Way Migration:

Projects under AGPL/GPLv3 may relicense under DACL (adding restrictions). Projects under DACL cannot relicense under GPL (removing restrictions violates copyleft). Commons can become more protective, never less.
